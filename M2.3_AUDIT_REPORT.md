# M2.3 VISION KNOWLEDGE DOMAIN | AUDIT REPORT

## 1. Enterprise Change Gate
This sprint is authorized ONLY to audit and model the "Vision Knowledge Domain" as an independent Enterprise Asset. No code modifications will be made. The current combined `VisionMissionPayload` structure remains technically unchanged during this sprint. Everything else (Front Matter, Regulatory Knowledge, School Knowledge, Canonical Renderer, etc.) remains strictly LOCKED.

## 2. Boundary Audit
- **Universal Renderer**: Untouched.
- **Publishing Engine**: Untouched.
- **Workspace**: Untouched.
- **Planning Engines**: Untouched.
- **Regulatory Knowledge**: Untouched.
- **School Knowledge**: Untouched.
- **Front Matter**: Untouched.
- **Mission Knowledge**: Untouched (Identified for decoupling).
- **Goals Knowledge**: Untouched (Identified for decoupling).

## 3. Vision Knowledge Audit
- **Purpose**: Defines the ultimate, long-term aspirational future state of the educational institution. It is the north star for all curriculum alignment and strategic planning.
- **Lifecycle**: Highly stable and long-lived. Typically defined once every 4-8 years and rarely changes during annual operational cycles.
- **Ownership**: The School's Governing Body, Foundation, or Principal.
- **Consumers**: KSP Document Builders (BAB II), RKT/RKAS Planning Engines, and Accreditation Authorities.

## 4. Knowledge Ownership Audit
- **School**: The true authoritative owner of the ideological content.
- **Government**: Not the owner, merely provides boundary constraints (e.g., must align with Pancasila).
- **Workspace**: Serves only as a temporary conduit/cache.
- **Future Registry**: Will act as the enterprise system of record and technical owner of the digital asset.

## 5. Knowledge Source Audit
- **Origin**: Vision Knowledge originates from a formal school decree (SK) or deliberation outside the system.
- **Current Flow**: Entered manually by users and passed through Workspace Metadata.
- **Future Flow**: Will be queried deterministically from the Future Registry. The system must never use AI to invent or assume a school's vision.

## 6. Knowledge Independence Audit
- **From Mission**: A Vision is the *destination*, while the Mission represents the *actions* taken to get there. A school may update its Mission (actions) without changing its Vision (destination).
- **From Goals**: Goals are measurable, short-to-medium-term milestones. Goals change annually; Vision does not.
- **From School Knowledge**: Physical infrastructure (classrooms) or demographics may shift, but the core Vision remains resilient.
- **From Regulatory Knowledge**: Government regulations frequently evolve, whereas a school's Vision is a foundational, stable identity.

## 7. Relationship Audit
**Dependency Direction**:
`Regulatory Knowledge` (Constraints) & `School Knowledge` (Current Reality) 
  ↓ influences
`Vision Knowledge` (Aspirational Future)
  ↓ drives
`Mission Knowledge` (Action Plan)
  ↓ measures
`Goals Knowledge` (Milestones)

When passed to the Builder, the Document (BAB II) simply projects the alignment of these independent domains.

## 8. Builder Audit
Builders must remain pure Consumers. A Builder will take the `VisionKnowledgePayload` and map it strictly to a `PARAGRAPH` or `QUOTE` Canonical Block. 
Builders shall never:
- Rewrite or "improve" the vision using AI.
- Infer a vision if one is missing.
- Summarize the vision.

## 9. Assembler Audit
The `KspDocumentAssembler` will act as the orchestrator:
1. Fetch `VisionKnowledgePayload`.
2. Fetch `MissionKnowledgePayload`.
3. Fetch `GoalsKnowledgePayload`.
4. Inject them as discrete, independent arguments into `buildKspBab2Section(vision, mission, goals)`.
The Assembler composes; the Builder projects.

## 10. Future Registry Audit
**Future Vision Knowledge Registry Design**:
- **Registry**: Exposes a standardized endpoint, e.g., `registry.getVision(schoolId)`.
- **Knowledge Object**: Returns a strongly-typed `CanonicalKnowledgeObject<VisionKnowledgeDomain>`.
- **Assembler**: Awaits the specific registry calls and passes the payloads downstream.
- **Builder**: Remains completely oblivious to the registry, consuming the exact same interface it does today and mapping to Canonical Blocks.

- `src/enterprise/knowledge/services/VisionMissionService.ts`: To split domain services.
- `src/enterprise/document/ksp/KspDocumentAssembler.ts`: To assemble domains independently.
- `src/enterprise/document/ksp/bab2/KspBab2Structure.ts`: To consume decoupled domains instead of a monolithic payload.

## 12. Risk Assessment
- **Architecture Risk**: **LOW**. Splitting a monolithic payload into discrete interfaces aligns perfectly with the established DDD architecture.
- **Knowledge Risk**: **LOW**. Strictly enforces domain boundaries.
- **Government Fidelity Risk**: **LOW**. Passthrough data behavior is maintained.
- **Regression Risk**: **MEDIUM**. Splitting the existing `VisionMission` interfaces will require careful refactoring across the Service, Builder, and Assembler layers to avoid breaking the BAB II rendering.
- **Future Registry Risk**: **LOW**. This decoupling is the exact prerequisite needed for micro-registry integration.

## 13. Implementation Strategy (NO CODE)
1. Define discrete interfaces: `VisionKnowledgeDomain`, `MissionKnowledgeDomain`, `GoalsKnowledgeDomain`.
2. Update the domain services and builders to return these discrete objects instead of a single `VisionMissionPayload`.
3. Refactor `KspDocumentAssembler` to compose them separately.
4. Refactor `buildKspBab2Section` to accept the decoupled objects.
5. Run lint and build to verify Zero Regression.
